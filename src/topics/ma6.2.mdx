An exchange rate is the price of one currency in terms of another. Reading it correctly, and knowing which currency got stronger, is the key skill for all of Unit 6.

## Reading an exchange rate

An exchange rate can be written either way:

- **$1.25 per euro** means one euro costs $1.25.
- **0.8 euro per dollar** means one dollar costs 0.8 euro.

These are the same rate. To flip one, divide 1 by it: 1 ÷ 1.25 = 0.8.

## Appreciation and depreciation

- A currency **appreciates** when it buys **more** of another currency. It is stronger.
- A currency **depreciates** when it buys **less**. It is weaker.

When one currency appreciates against another, the other must depreciate. If the dollar price of a euro rises from $1.10 to $1.25, the **euro appreciated** and the **dollar depreciated**.

## Converting prices

**Price in dollars = price in euros × dollars per euro**

If one dollar buys 0.8 euro, then a euro costs $1.25, and a €40 book costs €40 × $1.25 = $50.

## What a change means for trade

When the **dollar appreciates**:

- US goods cost more for foreigners, so **US exports fall**.
- Foreign goods cost less for Americans, so **US imports rise**.
- **US net exports fall.**

When the dollar depreciates, the reverse happens. Topic 6.5 connects this to aggregate demand.

  <Step n={1} title="Read the change">
    Each dollar now buys more yen (130 instead of 110), so the dollar appreciated and the yen depreciated.
  </Step>

    US goods now cost more yen, so Japanese buyers purchase fewer of them. US exports to Japan fall.

## On the exam

- Say which currency appreciated from a change in a rate.
- Convert a price from one currency to another.
- Predict how appreciation or depreciation affects exports, imports, and net exports.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Reading the rate backward. “$1.25 per euro” is the price of a euro. If that number rises, the euro got stronger, not the dollar.
</Callout>
