Inflation is a rise in the overall price level. To measure it, economists price a fixed basket of goods over time. This topic covers how the index is built, how to turn it into an inflation rate, and where it goes wrong.

## Price indices

A **price index** compares the cost of a fixed basket of goods with its cost in a **base year**:

**Price index = cost of the basket this year ÷ cost in the base year × 100**

The index is 100 in the base year by definition. The **consumer price index (CPI)** uses a basket of goods and services bought by a typical urban household. The **GDP deflator** covers everything in GDP (Topic 2.6).

## The inflation rate

**Inflation rate = (index this year − index last year) ÷ index last year × 100**

If the CPI rises from 150 to 156, inflation is 6 ÷ 150 = 4%.

- **Inflation:** the price level is rising.
- **Deflation:** the price level is falling (negative inflation).
- **Disinflation:** inflation is still positive but **slowing**, for example from 6% to 3%. Prices are still rising, just more slowly.

## Why the CPI overstates the cost of living

- **Substitution bias:** the basket is fixed, but people switch to cheaper goods when prices rise, so their actual cost rises less.
- **New products:** new goods enter the basket late.
- **Quality improvements:** some price increases buy a better product, which is not really inflation.

## Real values

A price index lets you compare dollars across years. To express a past amount in today’s dollars:

**Value today = past value × (index today ÷ index then)**

A **real wage** is the nominal wage adjusted for prices. If nominal wages rise 3% while prices rise 5%, the real wage falls about 2%.

## Worked example

<Worked title="Build an index" prompt="A basket has 10 pizzas and 5 movie tickets. In the base year pizzas cost $6 and tickets $8. This year pizzas cost $9 and tickets $10. Find the price index this year and the inflation since the base year.">
  <Step n={1} title="Base-year cost">
    10 × $6 + 5 × $8 = $100.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="This year’s cost">
    10 × $9 + 5 × $10 = $140.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Index and inflation">
    $140 ÷ $100 × 100 = 140. Prices rose 40% since the base year.
  </Step>
</Worked>

## On the exam

- Calculate a price index and an inflation rate.
- Tell inflation, deflation, and disinflation apart.
- Convert a nominal amount to real terms or across years.
- Name a reason the CPI overstates inflation.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Calling disinflation “falling prices.” During disinflation, prices are still rising, only more slowly. Falling prices are deflation.
</Callout>
