Unit 6 is about what happens when markets do not produce the best result for society. This topic gives you the test for “best” and the language for describing a failure.

## Allocative efficiency

A market is **allocatively efficient** when it produces the quantity where **marginal social benefit (MSB) equals marginal social cost (MSC)**. At that quantity, the value society places on the last unit equals what it costs society to make.

When there are no externalities, demand shows marginal benefit and supply shows marginal cost, so a **competitive market equilibrium is efficient**: P = MC. Total surplus is as large as it can be.

## When markets fail

A **market failure** happens when the market quantity is not the efficient quantity. The main causes in the course:

- **Market power** (monopoly, oligopoly, monopolistic competition): P > MC, so output is too low.
- **Externalities:** costs or benefits that fall on people outside the trade (Topic 6.2).
- **Public goods:** goods that markets underproduce because of free riders (Topic 6.3).
- **Government interventions** such as price controls and taxes can also move a market away from the efficient quantity (Topic 2.8).

## Deadweight loss

**Deadweight loss** is the total surplus lost when the quantity is not the efficient one. It is not a transfer from one group to another; it is value that simply disappears because mutually helpful trades do not happen, or wasteful ones do.

- **Too little output:** units whose MSB is above MSC are not made.
- **Too much output:** units whose MSC is above MSB are made anyway.

On a graph, deadweight loss is usually a triangle pointing toward the efficient quantity.

## Equity versus efficiency

Efficiency asks whether the size of the pie is as large as it can be. **Equity** asks whether it is divided fairly. A market can be efficient and still leave some people with very little. Governments sometimes accept some inefficiency to get a fairer outcome, which is a normative choice.

## On the exam

- Identify the efficient quantity where MSB = MSC.
- Say whether a market produces too much or too little, and shade the deadweight loss.
- Name the cause of a market failure in an example.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Counting a transfer as deadweight loss. Tax revenue or the extra profit a monopoly takes from buyers moves surplus from one group to another. Deadweight loss is only the surplus nobody gets.
</Callout>
