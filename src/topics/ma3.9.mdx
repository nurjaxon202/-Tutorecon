Some fiscal policy works without anyone voting on it. Automatic stabilizers are parts of the tax and spending system that push against recessions and booms on their own.

## What they are

An **automatic stabilizer** is a tax or spending program that changes automatically with the economy, in a direction that softens the business cycle. No new law is needed.

- **Progressive income taxes.** In a recession, incomes fall and many people drop into lower brackets, so tax payments fall faster than income. After-tax income falls less than pre-tax income, which cushions spending. In a boom, tax payments rise faster than income, cooling spending.
- **Unemployment insurance.** When people lose jobs, benefit payments rise automatically, supporting spending.
- **Means-tested transfers** such as food assistance. More families qualify in a recession, so payments rise.

## Effect on the budget

Because they work in the opposite direction of the cycle:

- In a **recession**, tax revenue falls and transfers rise, so the **budget deficit grows** automatically.
- In an **expansion**, tax revenue rises and transfers fall, so the **deficit shrinks**.

## Automatic versus discretionary

<div class="table-scroll">

| | Automatic stabilizers | Discretionary fiscal policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Needs a new law? | No | Yes |
| Lags | Very short | Recognition, decision, and implementation lags |
| Size | Softens swings | Can be sized to close a gap |
| Example | Unemployment benefits rise in a recession | A new stimulus bill |

</div>

Automatic stabilizers reduce the size of swings, but they rarely close a gap by themselves.

## Worked example

<Worked title="How much do stabilizers cushion a recession?" prompt="In a recession, household income before taxes falls by $100 billion. The average tax rate is 20%, and unemployment benefits and other transfers rise by $15 billion. By how much does disposable income fall?">
  <Step n={1} title="Taxes fall with income">
    Taxes drop by 20% of $100 billion, which is $20 billion.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Transfers rise">
    Benefits add $15 billion to what households receive.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Add it up">
    Disposable income falls by $100 billion − $20 billion − $15 billion = $65 billion.
  </Step>
  <Step n={4} title="Interpret">
    Without the stabilizers, disposable income would fall by the full $100 billion. They soften the drop by $35 billion without any new law, which props up consumption and aggregate demand.
  </Step>
</Worked>

## On the exam

- Identify an automatic stabilizer from an example.
- Explain how a progressive income tax stabilizes the economy.
- Predict what happens to the budget deficit in a recession with no new laws.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Calling a new stimulus bill an automatic stabilizer. If lawmakers had to act, it is discretionary. Automatic stabilizers are already built into the law.
</Callout>
