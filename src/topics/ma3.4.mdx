In the long run, wages and all other input prices catch up with the price level. Then output no longer depends on prices at all, and the aggregate supply curve becomes a vertical line at the economy’s potential.

## What LRAS shows

The **long-run aggregate supply (LRAS)** curve is **vertical** at **potential output**, also called **full-employment output**. It is the real GDP the economy makes when unemployment is at its natural rate.

## Why LRAS is vertical

In the long run, **nominal wages and other input prices are fully flexible**. If the price level doubles and wages double too, firms are no better off than before, so they have no reason to produce more. Output is set by the economy’s **resources and technology**, not by the price level.

## LRAS and the PPC

A point on LRAS matches a point **on** the production possibilities curve: all resources fully employed. The same things shift both.

## What shifts LRAS

LRAS shifts **right** when the economy’s capacity grows:

- More or better **resources**: labor force growth, immigration, new natural resources
- More **physical capital**: factories, machines, infrastructure
- More **human capital**: education and training
- Better **technology**
- Better institutions, such as strong property rights

It shifts **left** when capacity is lost: a war or natural disaster that destroys capital, or a large fall in the labor force.

A rightward shift of LRAS is **long-run economic growth** (Unit 5).

## In the long run, AD only moves prices

Because LRAS is vertical, an increase in AD in the long run raises only the **price level**, not real GDP. Output always returns to potential.
