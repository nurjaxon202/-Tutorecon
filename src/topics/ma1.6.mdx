AP Macro uses supply and demand for single markets in Unit 1, then reuses the same reasoning for the whole economy (aggregate demand and supply), the money market, loanable funds, and currencies. Getting it right here pays off in every later unit.

## Equilibrium and disequilibrium

The **equilibrium price** is where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.

- Above equilibrium, there is a **surplus**. Sellers cut prices to sell what they have.
- Below equilibrium, there is a **shortage**. Buyers bid the price up.

Markets move toward equilibrium unless a price control holds them away from it.

## Predicting changes

Use four steps: what changed, which curve moves, which way, and what happens to price and quantity.

## Why it matters for macro

- **Aggregate demand and supply** (Unit 3) work the same way, with the price level and real GDP on the axes.
- In the **money market** (Unit 4), the price of money is the nominal interest rate.
- In the **loanable funds market** (Unit 4), the price is the real interest rate.
- In the **foreign exchange market** (Unit 6), the price is the exchange rate.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Writing that a higher price shifts demand left. A change in a good’s own price moves along the curve. Only other factors shift it.
</Callout>
