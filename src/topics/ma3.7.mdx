If policymakers do nothing, does an economy in a gap fix itself? In the AD-AS model, yes, in the long run, because wages eventually adjust. This topic shows how, and why it can be slow.

## Closing a recessionary gap on its own

1. Output is below potential and unemployment is above the natural rate.
2. With many workers looking for jobs, **nominal wages** and other input prices **fall** over time.
3. Lower costs shift **SRAS to the right**.
4. Real GDP rises back to potential, and the price level falls further.

In the new long-run equilibrium, real GDP is **back at potential** and the price level is **lower** than before the recession began.

## Closing an inflationary gap on its own

1. Output is above potential and unemployment is below the natural rate.
2. Firms compete for scarce workers, and **nominal wages rise**.
3. Higher costs shift **SRAS to the left**.
4. Real GDP falls back to potential, and the price level rises further.

The new long-run equilibrium has real GDP at potential and a **higher price level**.

## Why it can take a long time

Wages are especially **sticky downward**. Contracts, minimum wage laws, and workers’ resistance to pay cuts slow the process. That is why many economists favor policy to close recessionary gaps sooner, while others worry that policy can overshoot.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Showing self-adjustment with a shift of AD. On its own, the economy corrects through wages and SRAS. AD shifts only if something else changes, such as policy.
</Callout>
