Put AD, SRAS, and LRAS on one graph and you have the main model of AP Macro. This topic is about reading it: finding equilibrium, spotting a gap, and naming what it means for unemployment and prices.

## Short-run equilibrium

Short-run equilibrium is where **AD crosses SRAS**. It gives the current **price level (PL)** and **real GDP (Y)**.

If the price level is above that point, firms produce more than people want to buy, and unsold goods push prices down. If it is below, spending exceeds output and prices are bid up.

## Long-run equilibrium

The economy is in **long-run equilibrium** when **AD, SRAS, and LRAS all cross at one point**. Output is at potential and unemployment equals the natural rate.

## Output gaps

- **Recessionary (negative) gap:** short-run equilibrium is to the **left** of LRAS. Real GDP is below potential, and unemployment is **above** the natural rate.
- **Inflationary (positive) gap:** short-run equilibrium is to the **right** of LRAS. Real GDP is above potential, and unemployment is **below** the natural rate. Prices are under upward pressure.

The size of the gap is the horizontal distance between current real GDP and potential GDP.

## On the exam

- Draw a correctly labeled AD-AS graph: price level on the vertical axis, real GDP on the horizontal, and the equilibrium values marked on both axes.
- Identify a recessionary or inflationary gap and its size.
- Say what the gap means for unemployment and the price level.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Labeling the axes “price” and “quantity.” On AD-AS graphs, the axes are the price level and real GDP (or real output). Graders look for those labels.
</Callout>
