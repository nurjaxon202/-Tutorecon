Every firm in every market structure uses the same rule to find its best output. Learn it once here and you will use it for perfect competition, monopoly, monopolistic competition, and factor markets.

## Marginal revenue

**Marginal revenue (MR)** is the extra revenue from selling one more unit: MR = ΔTR ÷ ΔQ.

- For a firm in **perfect competition**, the price stays the same no matter how much it sells, so **MR = P**.
- For a firm with market power, selling one more unit means lowering the price, so **MR is less than P** (Topic 4.2).

## The rule: MR = MC

A firm maximizes profit by producing where **marginal revenue equals marginal cost**.

- If MR > MC, the next unit adds more to revenue than to cost, so produce more.
- If MR < MC, the last unit cost more than it brought in, so produce less.
- At MR = MC, there is no change that raises profit.

When data come in whole units, the firm produces every unit for which MR is at least MC, and stops before the first unit where MC is greater than MR.

## Profit on a graph

Once the output is chosen, profit = (P − ATC) × Q. On a graph, it is a rectangle: the height is the gap between price and ATC at the chosen quantity, and the width is that quantity. If ATC is above the price, the same rectangle is a loss.

## On the exam

- Find the profit-maximizing output from a table, a graph, or MR and MC values.
- Decide whether to produce more or less when MR and MC differ.
- Shade the profit or loss rectangle on a graph.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Picking the output where total revenue is highest, or where ATC is lowest. Neither maximizes profit. Only MR = MC does.
</Callout>
