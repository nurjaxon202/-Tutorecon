Perfect competition is the benchmark every other market structure is compared with. The side-by-side graph of the market and one firm is on the AP exam nearly every year.

## The assumptions

- **Many buyers and sellers,** each too small to affect the price.
- **Identical (standardized) products.**
- **Easy entry and exit** in the long run.
- **Full information** about prices.

Farm products like wheat and corn are the usual examples.

## The firm is a price taker

The market price is set by market supply and demand. Each firm takes that price as given and can sell as much as it wants at it. So the firm’s demand curve is **horizontal (perfectly elastic)** at the market price, and **P = MR = AR = D** for the firm.

The firm produces where **P = MC**.

## Short run and long run

**Short run:** the firm can earn an economic profit (P > ATC), a loss (P < ATC), or break even.

**Long run:** profits draw new firms in, which shifts market supply right and lowers the price. Losses push firms out, which shifts market supply left and raises the price. This continues until **P = minimum ATC** and economic profit is zero.

## Efficiency

In long-run equilibrium a perfectly competitive firm is:

- **Allocatively efficient:** P = MC. The value of the last unit to buyers equals its cost.
- **Productively efficient:** P = minimum ATC. Goods are made at the lowest possible cost.

## Long-run industry supply

- **Constant-cost industry:** input prices do not change as the industry grows, so the long-run supply curve is **horizontal** at minimum ATC. After a demand change, the price returns to where it started.
- **Increasing-cost industry:** input prices rise as the industry grows, so long-run supply **slopes up**.
- **Decreasing-cost industry:** input prices fall as it grows, so long-run supply **slopes down**.

## Worked example

<Worked title="Demand rises in a constant-cost industry" prompt="A constant-cost, perfectly competitive industry is in long-run equilibrium. Demand for its product rises permanently. Trace the short run and the long run.">
  <Step n={1} title="Short run">
    Market demand shifts right, so the market price rises. Each firm moves up its MC curve, produces more, and earns an economic profit.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Entry">
    Profit attracts new firms. Market supply shifts right, pushing the price back down.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Long run">
    Entry stops when the price is back at minimum ATC, the original price. Each firm produces its original output, and there are more firms, so market quantity is higher.
  </Step>
</Worked>

## On the exam

- Draw side-by-side market and firm graphs with the price carried across.
- Show and calculate short-run profit or loss.
- Explain the adjustment to long-run equilibrium and why profit ends at zero.
- State that the firm is allocatively and productively efficient in the long run.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Drawing a downward-sloping demand curve for the individual firm. The market demand curve slopes down, but the single competitive firm faces a horizontal demand curve at the market price.
</Callout>
