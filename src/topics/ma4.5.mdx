The money market is where the nominal interest rate is set. Its graph is simple, and the exam uses it to show how monetary policy changes interest rates.

## Money demand

**Money demand (MD)** is how much money people want to hold. It **slopes down** against the **nominal interest rate**, because the interest rate is the **opportunity cost of holding money**: at a higher rate, holding cash means giving up more interest, so people hold less.

MD **shifts right** when people need more money for purchases:

- the **price level** rises,
- **real GDP** (income) rises.

It shifts left when those fall, or when payment technology lets people hold less cash.

## Money supply

**Money supply (MS)** is set by the central bank, so it is drawn as a **vertical** line. It does not depend on the interest rate.

## Equilibrium and changes

The **nominal interest rate** is where MS meets MD.

- **Money supply rises** (MS shifts right): the nominal interest rate **falls**.
- **Money supply falls** (MS shifts left): the rate **rises**.
- **Money demand rises** (MD shifts right, for example because of higher prices or income): the rate **rises**.
- **Money demand falls:** the rate **falls**.

## Why interest rates matter

A lower interest rate makes borrowing cheaper, which raises **investment** and interest-sensitive consumption such as car and home purchases. That raises aggregate demand. This is the first link in the chain from monetary policy to real GDP (Topic 4.6).

## Worked example

<Worked title="Find the interest rate" prompt="At a nominal interest rate of 6%, people want to hold $500 billion of money. At 4% they want $600 billion, and at 2% they want $700 billion. The money supply is $600 billion. What is the equilibrium interest rate? What happens if the central bank raises the money supply to $700 billion?">
  <Step n={1} title="Match supply and demand">
    Money demand equals the $600 billion supply at 4%, so the equilibrium nominal interest rate is 4%.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Raise the money supply">
    At 4%, people now hold $100 billion more than they want. They use it to buy bonds.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Follow the adjustment">
    Buying bonds raises bond prices and lowers interest rates until people want to hold $700 billion, which happens at 2%.
  </Step>
  <Step n={4} title="Connect to the economy">
    The lower rate makes borrowing cheaper, so investment and interest-sensitive spending rise and AD shifts right.
  </Step>
</Worked>

## On the exam

- Draw a money market with a vertical MS and downward-sloping MD, and label the nominal interest rate.
- Show a change in MS or MD and the new interest rate.
- Explain why MD slopes down.

Labeling the vertical axis “real interest rate.” The money market uses the nominal interest rate. The real rate belongs on the loanable funds graph.
