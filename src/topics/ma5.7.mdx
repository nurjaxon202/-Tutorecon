Governments cannot order an economy to grow, but their policies can make growth more or less likely. This topic covers which policies help, and the supply-side view of taxes.

## Policies that promote growth

- **Investing in human capital:** public education, job training, and health programs raise productivity.
- **Investing in physical capital (infrastructure):** roads, ports, power grids, and broadband make private businesses more productive. This spending raises AD in the short run and shifts LRAS right in the long run.
- **Supporting research and development:** research funding, grants, and tax credits for R&D speed up new technology.
- **Protecting property rights and enforcing contracts:** people invest more when they are sure they will keep the returns.
- **Encouraging saving and investment:** tax treatment that rewards saving and business investment adds to the capital stock.
- **Keeping inflation low and stable**, which makes long-term planning easier.
- **Open trade**, which lets a country specialize by comparative advantage.

## Supply-side fiscal policy

**Supply-side economists** argue that lower marginal tax rates on income and on investment raise the reward for working, saving, and investing. More labor and capital would shift **LRAS (and SRAS) to the right**, raising output without raising the price level as much as a demand-side stimulus would. Critics point out that tax cuts also raise AD and can widen budget deficits, and that the size of the supply response is debated.

## Policies that can slow growth

- Large, persistent deficits that **crowd out** private investment
- Weak property rights, corruption, and political instability
- High, unpredictable inflation

## On the exam

- Name a policy that shifts LRAS right and explain the mechanism.
- Distinguish a policy’s short-run effect on AD from its long-run effect on LRAS.
- Describe the supply-side argument for lower marginal tax rates.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Answering “increase government spending” as a growth policy with no detail. Spending on consumption does little for long-run growth. It is spending on human capital, infrastructure, and research that shifts LRAS.
</Callout>
