A business can show a healthy profit on its books and still be a bad use of its owner’s time and money. The difference is implicit costs, and it decides whether firms enter or leave an industry.

## Three kinds of profit

- **Accounting profit = total revenue − explicit costs.** What a business reports for taxes.
- **Economic profit = total revenue − explicit costs − implicit costs.** Economists subtract the opportunity cost of everything the owner puts in: their time, their building, their savings.
- **Normal profit** is what economists call zero economic profit. The firm earns exactly enough to cover all its costs, including the owner’s next best alternative. The owner has no reason to leave.

Because implicit costs are never negative, economic profit is always less than or equal to accounting profit.

## Why economic profit matters

Economic profit is the signal for entry and exit:

- **Positive economic profit** means resources earn more here than elsewhere, so new firms want in.
- **Economic losses** (negative economic profit) mean resources would earn more elsewhere, so firms eventually leave.
- **Zero economic profit** means no one has a reason to enter or exit. This is the long-run result in perfect competition and monopolistic competition.

## Worked example

<Worked title="The lawyer’s bakery" prompt="A lawyer leaves a $120,000 job to open a bakery. It brings in $200,000 and pays $110,000 in explicit costs. Find accounting profit and economic profit. Should the owner stay?">
  <Step n={1} title="Accounting profit">
    $200,000 − $110,000 = $90,000.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Economic profit">
    $90,000 − $120,000 (the salary given up) = −$30,000, an economic loss.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Decision">
    On money alone, the owner would be $30,000 better off back at the law firm. Unless running the bakery is worth that much to them in other ways, they should leave.
  </Step>
</Worked>

## On the exam

- Calculate accounting and economic profit from a story.
- Explain that zero economic profit is still a normal return for the owner.
- Use economic profit to predict entry and exit.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Thinking zero economic profit means the business is failing. It means the owner earns exactly what they could earn in their next best option, which is enough to stay open.
</Callout>
