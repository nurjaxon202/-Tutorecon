Demand describes buyers. Almost every question in Unit 2, and many in later units, begins by asking what happened to demand, so the difference between a shift and a movement has to be automatic.

## What you need to know

**Demand** is the whole set of quantities buyers are willing and able to buy at each possible price, in a given time. The **law of demand** says that, other things equal, a higher price lowers the **quantity demanded** and a lower price raises it. That is why the demand curve slopes down.

Three reasons the curve slopes down:

- **Substitution effect.** When a good’s price rises, other goods look cheaper next to it, so some buyers switch.
- **Income effect.** A higher price means the same income buys less, so buyers can afford less.
- **Diminishing marginal utility.** Each extra unit is worth a little less, so buyers take more only at a lower price.

## Movement or shift

A change in the good’s **own price** moves buyers **along** the demand curve. That is a change in **quantity demanded**.

A change in anything else that affects buyers **shifts** the entire curve. That is a change in **demand**. The five shifters spell TRIBE:

- **Tastes.** A product goes viral, or a study says it is unhealthy.
- **Related goods.** If a **substitute** gets more expensive, demand for this good rises. If a **complement** gets more expensive, demand for this good falls.
- **Income.** For a **normal good**, more income raises demand. For an **inferior good**, like bus rides or instant noodles for many people, more income lowers demand.
- **Buyers.** More people in the market raises demand.
- **Expectations.** If buyers expect a higher price or higher income soon, demand rises now.

An increase in demand shifts the curve to the **right**: buyers want more at every price. A decrease shifts it to the **left**.

## Worked example

<Worked title="Shift or move?" prompt="Classify each event for the market for coffee: (a) the price of coffee rises; (b) the price of tea, a substitute, falls; (c) a study says coffee improves memory; (d) the price of sugar, a complement, rises.">
  <Step n={1} title="(a) Own price">
    A change in coffee’s own price is a movement along the curve. Quantity demanded falls. Demand does not change.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="(b) Cheaper substitute">
    Tea is now more attractive, so demand for coffee falls. The curve shifts left.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="(c) Tastes">
    Buyers like coffee more, so demand rises. The curve shifts right.
  </Step>
  <Step n={4} title="(d) Pricier complement">
    Coffee with sugar now costs more in total, so demand for coffee falls. The curve shifts left.
  </Step>
</Worked>

## On the exam

- Decide whether an event shifts demand, and which way.
- Identify substitutes, complements, normal goods, and inferior goods from how demand responds.
- In FRQs, draw the new demand curve, label it D₂, and show the new price and quantity on the axes.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Writing “the price rose, so demand fell.” A price change never shifts its own good’s demand curve. The correct phrase is “quantity demanded fell.”
</Callout>
