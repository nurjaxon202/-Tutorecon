Price elasticity of supply measures how strongly sellers respond when the price changes. It explains why the price of concert tickets jumps when demand rises, while the price of pencils barely moves.

## What you need to know

**Price elasticity of supply (Es)** is the percentage change in quantity supplied divided by the percentage change in price:

**Es = %ΔQs ÷ %ΔP**

Because price and quantity supplied move in the same direction, Es is positive.

- **Es > 1: elastic.** Sellers can raise output a lot when the price rises.
- **Es < 1: inelastic.** Output is hard to change quickly.
- **Es = 0: perfectly inelastic.** A vertical supply curve. The quantity is fixed, like seats in one stadium on game day or paintings by an artist who has died.
- **Es = ∞: perfectly elastic.** A horizontal supply curve. Sellers will supply any amount at one price.

## What makes supply more elastic

- **Time.** The most important factor. In the very short run, output is often fixed. Over months, firms add shifts and workers. Over years, they build factories and new firms enter.
- **Spare capacity and easy-to-get inputs.** A factory running below capacity can raise output quickly.
- **Mobile resources.** If workers and machines can switch easily from one product to another, supply is more elastic.
- **Storage.** Goods that can be stored cheaply can be released when prices rise.

## Why it matters

When demand increases, supply elasticity decides what changes more:

- With **inelastic** supply, most of the increase in demand shows up as a **higher price**.
- With **elastic** supply, most of it shows up as a **higher quantity**, and the price barely moves.

That is why the price of fresh fish at a dock can jump in one afternoon, while the price of a mass-produced toy stays steady even when it becomes popular.

## Worked example

<Worked title="Compute and classify" prompt="The price of a good rises 20%, and producers raise the quantity supplied by 30%. What is the price elasticity of supply?">
  <Step n={1} title="Divide">
    30% ÷ 20% = 1.5.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Classify">
    1.5 is greater than 1, so supply is elastic. Sellers respond more than proportionally to the price change.
  </Step>
</Worked>
