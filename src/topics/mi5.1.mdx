Factor markets are where firms buy the inputs they need: labor, land, and capital. Most of the unit is about labor markets, but the same logic applies to every input.

## Who buys and who sells

In **product markets**, households buy goods and services from firms. In **factor markets**, the roles flip: **households sell** labor, land, and capital, and **firms buy** them. The payments are wages (labor), rent (land), interest (capital), and profit (entrepreneurship). This is the circular flow from Unit 1 in action.

## Derived demand

The demand for a factor is a **derived demand**: firms want workers not for their own sake but for what they help produce. The demand for pilots comes from the demand for air travel. If people fly less, airlines need fewer pilots.

## Marginal revenue product

The value of one more worker to a firm is the **marginal revenue product of labor (MRP)**:

**MRP = marginal product (MP) × marginal revenue (MR)**

For a firm selling in a perfectly competitive product market, MR equals the price of the product, so **MRP = MP × P**.

Because of diminishing marginal returns, MP falls as more workers are hired, so MRP falls too. **The MRP curve is the firm’s demand curve for labor.**

## Marginal factor cost

The cost of one more worker is the **marginal factor cost (MFC)**, sometimes called marginal resource cost. In a **competitive labor market**, the firm can hire as many workers as it wants at the market wage, so **MFC = wage**, and the labor supply curve the firm faces is horizontal.

The hiring rule, **MRP = MFC**, is the factor-market version of MR = MC. Topic 5.3 puts it to work.

## Worked example

<Worked title="How many workers to hire" prompt="A firm sells its product for $5 in a competitive market and pays a wage of $32 a day. The first four workers add 10, 8, 6, and 4 units of output a day. How many workers should it hire?">
  <Step n={1} title="Find each worker’s MRP">
    MRP = marginal product × price: $50, $40, $30, and $20.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Compare with the wage">
    The first worker adds $50 and the second adds $40, both above the $32 wage. The third adds only $30.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Answer">
    Hire 2 workers. The third would cost $32 and bring in only $30.
  </Step>
  <Step n={4} title="The idea behind it">
    The firm’s demand for labor comes from the value of what workers make. If the product’s price rose, every MRP would rise and the firm would hire more.
  </Step>
</Worked>

<Callout kind="mistake">
Reversing the roles. In factor markets, firms are the buyers and households are the sellers. The money flows from firms to households as wages and other factor payments.
</Callout>
