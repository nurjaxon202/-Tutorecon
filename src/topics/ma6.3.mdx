Exchange rates in a floating system are set by supply and demand in the foreign exchange market. The graph is ordinary supply and demand, but the labels take care.

## Setting up the graph

In the market for a currency, say US dollars:

- The **horizontal axis** is the **quantity of dollars**.
- The **vertical axis** is the **price of a dollar in another currency**, for example euros per dollar.

## Who demands and who supplies

**Demand for dollars** comes from people who need dollars to buy US things:

- foreigners buying **US exports**,
- foreign tourists visiting the US,
- foreigners buying **US assets** such as stocks, bonds, and property.

**Supply of dollars** comes from people who have dollars and want another currency:

- Americans buying **imports**,
- American tourists going abroad,
- Americans buying **foreign assets**.

The two markets mirror each other. In the market for euros (priced in dollars), Americans who want European goods **demand euros** and **supply dollars**.

## Shifts and results

- **Demand for a currency rises:** it **appreciates**.
- **Supply of a currency rises:** it **depreciates**.

## Worked example

<Worked title="American tourists in Mexico" prompt="American tourists travel to Mexico in record numbers. What happens in the market for Mexican pesos, and in the market for US dollars?">
  <Step n={1} title="Pesos">
    Tourists need pesos, so demand for pesos rises. The peso appreciates.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Dollars">
    To get pesos, tourists sell dollars, so the supply of dollars rises. The dollar depreciates against the peso.
  </Step>
</Worked>

## On the exam

- Draw a correctly labeled foreign exchange graph, with the quantity of the currency on the horizontal axis and its price in the other currency on the vertical axis.
- Decide whether an event shifts demand or supply for a currency.
- Show the matching change in the market for the other currency.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Labeling the vertical axis “price.” It must say what the currency is priced in, such as “euros per dollar.” A bare “price” does not tell the grader which currency you mean.
</Callout>
