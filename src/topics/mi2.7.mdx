This topic is where supply and demand start doing real work: predicting what happens to price and quantity when something in the world changes. The four-step method below handles every case, including the hard ones where both curves move.

## Surpluses and shortages

When the price is **above** equilibrium, sellers offer more than buyers want. The gap is a **surplus**. Unsold goods pile up, and sellers cut prices until the market clears.

When the price is **below** equilibrium, buyers want more than sellers offer. The gap is a **shortage**. Buyers compete for the goods, and the price is bid up.

Either way, the market moves back toward equilibrium unless something, like a price control, stops it.

## The four moves

1. **What changed?** Name the event in economic terms: an input cost, a tastes change, a new technology.
2. **Which curve?** Buyers’ reasons shift demand. Sellers’ costs and numbers shift supply.
3. **Which way?** Right for an increase, left for a decrease.
4. **What happens?** Read the new equilibrium price and quantity.

## One curve shifts

| Change | Price | Quantity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demand increases | Rises | Rises |
| Demand decreases | Falls | Falls |
| Supply increases | Falls | Rises |
| Supply decreases | Rises | Falls |

## Both curves shift

When both curves shift, one of price or quantity always moves in a clear direction, and the other is **indeterminate**: it depends on which shift is bigger.

| Change | Price | Quantity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demand up, supply up | Indeterminate | Rises |
| Demand down, supply down | Indeterminate | Falls |
| Demand up, supply down | Rises | Indeterminate |
| Demand down, supply up | Falls | Indeterminate |

A quick way to check: work out each shift alone. If both push a variable the same way, that direction is certain. If they push it opposite ways, it is indeterminate.

## Worked example

<Worked title="A double shift" prompt="A new process lowers the cost of making laptops. At the same time, consumer incomes fall, and laptops are a normal good. What happens to the price and quantity of laptops?">
  <Step n={1} title="Supply">
    Lower costs shift supply right: price down, quantity up.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Demand">
    Lower incomes shift demand for a normal good left: price down, quantity down.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Combine">
    Both push the price down, so the price falls for certain. Quantity is pushed up by one and down by the other, so it is indeterminate.
  </Step>
</Worked>

## On the exam

- Predict the change in price and quantity from one or two shifts.
- Name what is indeterminate in a double shift and explain why.
- In FRQs, draw the shift, label the new curve and equilibrium, and show the new price and quantity with dashed lines to the axes.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Guessing an answer for the indeterminate variable. If the question does not say which shift is larger, the right answer is that the change cannot be determined.
</Callout>
