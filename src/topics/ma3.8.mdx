Fiscal policy is the government’s use of spending and taxes to steer the economy. It is the first of the two big policy tools in AP Macro, and FRQs often ask you to pick the right one, say how big it should be, and show its effect.

## Expansionary and contractionary

**Expansionary fiscal policy** raises aggregate demand to close a **recessionary gap**:

- increase government purchases,
- cut taxes,
- increase transfer payments.

**Contractionary fiscal policy** lowers aggregate demand to close an **inflationary gap**:

- cut government purchases,
- raise taxes,
- reduce transfer payments.

Expansionary policy raises real GDP and the price level and lowers unemployment in the short run. Contractionary policy does the opposite.

## Size with the multipliers

- Change in government purchases needed = gap ÷ spending multiplier
- Change in taxes needed = gap ÷ tax multiplier (in size)

## Effects on the budget

Expansionary fiscal policy **increases the budget deficit** (or shrinks a surplus). Contractionary policy reduces the deficit. Unit 5 covers how deficits add to the national debt and can **crowd out** private investment by raising interest rates.

## Lags

**Discretionary** fiscal policy needs new laws, so it faces delays:

- **Recognition lag:** seeing that there is a problem.
- **Decision (legislative) lag:** Congress debating and passing a bill.
- **Implementation lag:** getting the money spent or the tax change in effect.

By the time the policy works, the economy may have changed.

## Worked example

<Worked title="Inflationary gap" prompt="The MPC is 0.8 and there is an inflationary gap of $100 billion. By how much should government spending change? By how much should taxes change instead?">
  <Step n={1} title="Multipliers">
    MPS = 0.2. Spending multiplier = 5. Tax multiplier = −4.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Spending">
    Cut government purchases by $100 billion ÷ 5 = $20 billion.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Taxes">
    Raise taxes by $100 billion ÷ 4 = $25 billion.
  </Step>
</Worked>

## On the exam

- Pick the right fiscal policy for a gap and name a specific action.
- Calculate the size of the change using the multipliers.
- Show the AD shift and state the effects on real GDP, the price level, and unemployment.
- Name a lag that affects fiscal policy.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Including the central bank in fiscal policy. Fiscal policy is Congress and the president: spending and taxes. Changing the money supply or interest rates is monetary policy.
</Callout>
