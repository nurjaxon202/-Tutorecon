The Phillips curve shows the trade-off between inflation and unemployment in the short run, and the lack of one in the long run. It is the AD-AS model drawn with different axes.

## The short-run Phillips curve

The **short-run Phillips curve (SRPC)** slopes **down**: lower unemployment comes with higher inflation, and higher unemployment with lower inflation. The axes are the **inflation rate** (vertical) and the **unemployment rate** (horizontal).

The link to AD-AS:

- **AD shifts** cause movements **along** the SRPC. More AD means more output, lower unemployment, and higher inflation, so the economy moves **up and to the left**. Less AD moves it down and to the right.
- **SRAS shifts** cause **shifts of** the SRPC. A negative supply shock (SRAS left) raises both inflation and unemployment, so the **SRPC shifts right**. A positive supply shock shifts it left.

## The long-run Phillips curve

The **long-run Phillips curve (LRPC)** is **vertical at the natural rate of unemployment**. It matches the vertical LRAS.

In the long run, people adjust their **expectations of inflation**. If inflation stays high, workers demand higher wages and firms raise prices, so any attempt to keep unemployment below the natural rate just produces ever-higher inflation. There is **no long-run trade-off**.

## Expectations shift the SRPC

- **Higher expected inflation** shifts the SRPC **right (up)**.
- **Lower expected inflation** shifts it **left (down)**.

## What shifts the LRPC

The LRPC moves only when the **natural rate of unemployment** changes. Better job-matching technology or training programs lower the natural rate and shift the LRPC **left**. Changes that raise frictional or structural unemployment shift it right.

## Worked example

<Worked title="A policy that runs too hot" prompt="An economy is at its natural rate of 5% unemployment with 2% inflation. The central bank expands the money supply to push unemployment down to 3%. What happens in the short run and in the long run?">
  <Step n={1} title="Short run">
    Higher AD moves the economy up along the SRPC: unemployment falls to 3% and inflation rises.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Expectations">
    People come to expect the higher inflation, so the SRPC shifts right.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Long run">
    Unemployment returns to the natural rate of 5%, but inflation stays higher than the original 2%.
  </Step>
</Worked>

## On the exam

- Draw and label SRPC and LRPC with the correct axes.
- Show an AD change as a movement along the SRPC and a supply shock as a shift of it.
- Explain why the LRPC is vertical and what shifts it.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Shifting the SRPC when AD changes. A change in AD moves the economy along the short-run Phillips curve. The curve itself shifts only with supply shocks or changes in expected inflation.
</Callout>
