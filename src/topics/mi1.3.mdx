The production possibilities curve (PPC) is the first graph in both courses and one of the most tested. It shows, on one picture, scarcity, choice, opportunity cost, efficiency, and growth.

## What you need to know

A **production possibilities curve** shows every combination of two goods an economy can make when it uses all of its resources and its current technology fully and efficiently.

- A point **on** the curve is **efficient**: the economy cannot make more of one good without making less of the other.
- A point **inside** the curve is **inefficient**: resources are idle or badly used, for example during a recession with high unemployment.
- A point **outside** the curve is **unattainable** with today’s resources and technology.

Moving along the curve from one point to another shows the **opportunity cost** of the choice: how much of one good must be given up to get more of the other.

## Shape: straight or bowed out

A **straight-line PPC** means **constant opportunity cost**. Every extra unit of one good costs the same amount of the other. This happens when resources are equally good at making both goods.

A PPC **bowed outward** (concave to the origin) means **increasing opportunity cost**. As an economy makes more of one good, it has to use resources that are less and less suited to it, so each extra unit costs more of the other good. Most real economies look like this.

## What shifts the curve

The curve shifts **outward** when the economy can make more of both goods:

- More resources: population growth, immigration, new oil deposits
- More capital: new factories, machines, and infrastructure
- Better technology or more human capital: new methods, better training

It shifts **inward** when resources are lost, as after a war, a natural disaster, or a disease that reduces the workforce.

If a change affects only one good, such as a new farming method, the curve **pivots**: it moves out along that good’s axis only.

Unemployment does **not** shift the curve. It moves the economy to a point inside it.

## Capital goods today, growth tomorrow

An economy that puts more resources into capital goods (machines, factories) and fewer into consumer goods today will usually see its PPC shift out faster in the future. The trade-off is less consumption now for more production later.

## On the exam

- Label a point as efficient, inefficient, or unattainable.
- Calculate opportunity cost from a table or graph. Remember to state it as units of the good given up.
- Show growth with an outward shift and name a cause. FRQs often ask you to draw the new curve and label it.
- Explain why a curve is bowed outward: resources are not equally suited to both goods.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Saying unemployment shifts the PPC inward. The economy still has the same workers and machines, it just is not using them, so it sits inside the curve. The curve shifts inward only when resources are actually lost.
</Callout>
