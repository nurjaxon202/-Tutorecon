Nominal GDP can rise just because prices went up. Real GDP strips out price changes so you can see whether the economy actually produced more.

## Nominal and real GDP

- **Nominal GDP** values this year’s output at **this year’s prices**.
- **Real GDP** values this year’s output at **base-year prices**, holding prices constant.

In the base year, nominal and real GDP are equal. After the base year, when prices usually rise, nominal GDP is usually larger than real GDP.

## The GDP deflator

**GDP deflator = nominal GDP ÷ real GDP × 100**

Rearranged: **real GDP = nominal GDP ÷ deflator × 100**

If nominal GDP is $12 trillion and the deflator is 120, real GDP is $12 trillion ÷ 1.20 = $10 trillion.

The deflator covers every good in GDP, including investment and government purchases, while the CPI covers only a consumer basket, which can include imports.

## Growth rates

**Economic growth** is measured with **real GDP**. A quick approximation:

**% change in real GDP ≈ % change in nominal GDP − inflation rate**

If nominal GDP grows 6% and prices rise 4%, real GDP grows about 2%.

## On the exam

- Convert nominal GDP to real GDP with the deflator, or find the deflator.
- Calculate real GDP from a table using base-year prices.
- Explain why real GDP is used to measure growth.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Using this year’s prices for real GDP. Real GDP always uses base-year prices. Only the quantities come from this year.
</Callout>
