Money is anything widely accepted as payment. That sounds simple, but the exam asks what money does, what it is made of today, and how it is measured.

## Three functions of money

- **Medium of exchange:** used to buy and sell. It removes the need for a **double coincidence of wants**, the barter problem where each side must want exactly what the other has.
- **Unit of account:** a common measure of value. Prices are stated in dollars, so you can compare a phone and a laptop directly.
- **Store of value:** it holds value over time, so you can earn now and spend later. Inflation weakens this function.

## Commodity and fiat money

- **Commodity money** has value on its own, like gold or silver coins.
- **Fiat money** has value because the government declares it legal tender and people trust that others will accept it. The US dollar is fiat money. It is not backed by gold.

## Measuring the money supply

The Federal Reserve measures money in categories based on liquidity. In the AP course:

- **M1:** the most liquid money: currency held by the public and checkable (checking) deposits, plus traveler’s checks.
- **M2:** M1 plus near-money: savings deposits, small time deposits (CDs under $100,000), and money market mutual funds.

Stocks, bonds, and houses are **assets**, not money. A **credit card is not money** either. It is a way to take out a short-term loan.

## Moving between categories

Moving money from checking to savings lowers M1 but leaves M2 the same, because savings are already in M2. (In 2020 the Federal Reserve began counting savings deposits in M1 in its own statistics. Exam questions that give definitions expect you to use the definitions they give.) Withdrawing cash from a checking account leaves M1 unchanged at first: currency rises and deposits fall by the same amount.

  <Step n={3} title="The certificate of deposit">

## On the exam

- Match an example to a function of money.
- Classify items as M1, M2, or not money.
- Explain why fiat money has value.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Counting a credit card as money. When you pay with a card, the card company lends you the money. The money is the balance you later use to pay the bill.
</Callout>
