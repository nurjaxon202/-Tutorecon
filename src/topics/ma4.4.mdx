Banks do not just store money. When they make loans, they create new money. This topic covers the bank balance sheet, reserves, and the money multiplier, which appear in free-response questions almost every year.

## Fractional reserve banking

Banks keep only a fraction of deposits as **reserves** and lend out the rest. Under a **reserve requirement**, a bank must hold at least a set percentage of its checkable deposits as reserves.

- **Required reserves = deposits × reserve requirement**
- **Excess reserves = total reserves − required reserves**

A single bank can safely lend out its **excess reserves**.

## The balance sheet (T-account)

- **Assets** (left): reserves, loans, government bonds (securities), property.
- **Liabilities and owner’s equity** (right): deposits, and the owners’ stake.

Assets must equal liabilities plus owner’s equity.

## How loans create money

When a bank makes a loan, it usually adds the amount to the borrower’s checking account. That new deposit **is money**, so the money supply rises. When the borrower spends it, the money is deposited in another bank, which keeps part as reserves and lends out the rest, and the process repeats.

## The money multiplier

**Money multiplier = 1 ÷ reserve requirement**

- With a 10% requirement, the multiplier is 10.
- **Maximum change in the money supply = excess reserves created × money multiplier**

Two cases to handle carefully:

- A **cash deposit** already counted in M1 does not change M1 at first (currency falls, deposits rise). The potential **new** money comes only from lending the excess reserves.
- When the **Fed buys bonds from the public**, the seller’s new deposit adds to M1 right away, and the excess reserves it creates can be lent out and multiplied.

The actual multiplier is usually **smaller** than the simple one, because banks may hold extra reserves and people hold some money as cash instead of depositing it.

## Worked example

<Worked title="A new deposit" prompt="The reserve requirement is 20%. Someone deposits $1,000 in cash into a checking account. How much can this bank lend? What is the most the money supply can change, including the whole banking system?">
  <Step n={1} title="Required reserves">
    20% of $1,000 = $200.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Excess reserves">
    $1,000 − $200 = $800. This bank can lend $800.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="System-wide">
    Money multiplier = 1 ÷ 0.2 = 5. Maximum new money = $800 × 5 = $4,000. The $1,000 deposit itself was already money as cash, so it adds nothing new.
  </Step>
</Worked>

## On the exam

- Fill in a T-account and find required and excess reserves.
- Calculate how much one bank can lend and how much the system can create.
- Explain why the real multiplier is smaller than the simple one.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Multiplying the whole deposit by the multiplier. Only excess reserves can be lent out, so multiply the excess reserves, not the full deposit.
</Callout>
