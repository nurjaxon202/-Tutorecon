AP Macro opens with the same tools as AP Micro, but uses them to talk about a whole economy: what it can produce, what each choice costs, and what makes it grow.

## Opportunity cost

**Opportunity cost** is the value of the next best alternative given up. For a country, choosing more of one good means using resources that could have made something else. A government that spends more on defense gives up the roads, schools, or private goods those resources could have made.

## The production possibilities curve

A **production possibilities curve (PPC)** shows the combinations of two goods an economy can make when it uses all its resources fully and efficiently with its current technology.

- **On the curve:** efficient, full employment.
- **Inside the curve:** some resources are unemployed or misused, as in a recession.
- **Outside the curve:** not possible today.

The curve’s **shape** shows opportunity cost. A straight line means constant opportunity cost. A curve bowed outward means **increasing opportunity cost**, because resources are not equally suited to both goods.

## The PPC and macroeconomics

The PPC ties directly to later Macro units:

- A **recession** moves the economy to a point **inside** the curve. Cyclical unemployment means idle workers.
- **Economic growth** shifts the curve **outward**. The causes are more resources, more capital, more human capital, and better technology, which are the same things that shift long-run aggregate supply (Unit 3) and drive growth (Unit 5).
- An economy that makes more **capital goods** today gives up some consumer goods now but can expect its PPC to shift out faster in the future.

## On the exam

- Identify efficient, inefficient, and unattainable points.
- Show a recession (a point inside the curve) and growth (an outward shift), and name what causes each.
- Calculate opportunity cost from a graph or table.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Showing a recession as an inward shift of the PPC. A recession leaves the economy’s resources in place but idle, so it moves the economy inside the curve. The curve shifts inward only when resources are destroyed or lost.
</Callout>
