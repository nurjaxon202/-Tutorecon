The balance of payments records every transaction between one country and the rest of the world. It has two main parts, and they must balance.

## The current account

The **current account** records trade in goods and services and flows of income:

- **Exports and imports of goods** (the balance of trade)
- **Exports and imports of services**, such as tourism, shipping, and software
- **Net income from abroad**: interest, dividends, and wages earned from other countries minus those paid to them
- **Net transfers**: foreign aid and money sent home by workers abroad (remittances)

Money flowing **in** (exports, income received) is a **credit**. Money flowing **out** (imports, income paid) is a **debit**.

## The financial account

The **financial account** (sometimes called the capital account in older materials) records the purchase and sale of **assets** between countries: stocks, bonds, real estate, and factories.

- When foreigners buy US assets, money flows into the US. That is a **capital inflow**, a credit.
- When Americans buy foreign assets, money flows out. That is a **capital outflow**, a debit.

A separate, small **capital account** records items like debt forgiveness and transfers of patents. The AP exam usually treats it as zero.

## They balance

**Current account + capital account + financial account = 0**

A country with a **current account deficit** (it buys more from the world than it sells) must have a **financial account surplus** (it sells assets to, or borrows from, the rest of the world) to pay for it.

## Worked example

<Worked title="Find the balances" prompt="A country has exports of goods $300 billion, imports of goods $450 billion, exports of services $120 billion, imports of services $70 billion, net income from abroad +$20 billion, and net transfers −$10 billion. Find the balance of trade in goods, the current account, and the financial account, assuming the capital account is zero.">
  <Step n={1} title="Goods">
    $300 − $450 = −$150 billion.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Current account">
    −$150 + ($120 − $70) + $20 − $10 = −$90 billion.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Financial account">
    It balances the current account: +$90 billion.
  </Step>
</Worked>

## On the exam

- Place a transaction in the current account or the financial account, and call it a credit or a debit.
- Calculate the balance of trade and the current account from a table.
- Explain why a current account deficit comes with a financial account surplus.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Putting a foreign company’s purchase of a US factory in the current account. Buying an asset is recorded in the financial account. The current account is for goods, services, income, and transfers.
</Callout>
