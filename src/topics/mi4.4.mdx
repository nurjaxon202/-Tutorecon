Restaurants, hair salons, clothing brands, and coffee shops are monopolistic competition: many sellers, each a little different. It mixes features of monopoly and perfect competition, and its long-run graph is a common free-response question.

## The features

- **Many firms**, each small compared with the market.
- **Differentiated products:** different quality, style, location, or service. Each firm has a little market power.
- **Easy entry and exit.**
- **Non-price competition:** firms compete with advertising, branding, and product features as well as price.

## Short run

Each firm faces its own downward-sloping demand curve. It produces where **MR = MC** and charges the price on its demand curve, just like a monopolist. It can earn an economic profit or take a loss in the short run.

## Long run

Because entry is easy:

- **Profits attract new firms.** Customers are spread among more sellers, so each firm’s demand curve shifts **left** (and gets more elastic) until profit is zero.
- **Losses drive firms out.** Each remaining firm’s demand shifts **right** until losses end.

In long-run equilibrium, each firm’s demand curve is **tangent to its ATC curve** at the profit-maximizing quantity, so **P = ATC** and economic profit is **zero**.

## Efficiency

In the long run, a firm in monopolistic competition is:

- **Not allocatively efficient:** P > MC.
- **Not productively efficient:** it produces on the falling part of ATC, not at the minimum.
- It has **excess capacity**: it could lower its average cost by producing more.

The trade-off is **product variety**, which buyers value.

## Advertising

Firms advertise to **shift their demand curve right** and make it **less elastic**, by convincing buyers their product has no close substitutes. Advertising also raises costs, shifting ATC up.

## On the exam

- Draw the long-run graph: demand tangent to ATC at the quantity where MR = MC.
- Explain how entry or exit moves each firm’s demand curve.
- Compare with perfect competition: zero profit in both, but P > MC and excess capacity here.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Drawing the long-run tangency at the bottom of ATC. The tangency is on the downward-sloping part of ATC, to the left of its minimum. That gap is excess capacity.
</Callout>
