Topic 2.9 puts a country’s own supply and demand next to the world price. It shows who wins and who loses from free trade, and what a tariff or quota changes.

## Opening to trade: importing

Suppose the **world price** is below the country’s own (domestic) equilibrium price. When the country opens to free trade, the price falls to the world price.

- Domestic buyers purchase more, so **consumer surplus rises**.
- Domestic producers sell less, so **producer surplus falls**.
- The gap between domestic quantity demanded and domestic quantity supplied is **imports**.
- Consumers gain more than producers lose, so **total surplus rises**.

## Opening to trade: exporting

If the world price is **above** the domestic price, the country exports. Producers gain, domestic buyers pay more and lose, and total surplus rises again because producers’ gains are larger.

## Tariffs

A **tariff** is a tax on imports. It raises the domestic price above the world price by the amount of the tariff.

- Domestic production rises and imports fall.
- Consumer surplus falls. Producer surplus rises.
- The government collects **tariff revenue** = tariff × quantity imported after the tariff.
- Two triangles of **deadweight loss** appear: one from producing at home units that could be imported more cheaply, and one from buyers who stop buying.

## Import quotas

A **quota** caps the quantity that can be imported. Its effects on price, domestic production, and deadweight loss are like a tariff’s, but instead of tariff revenue, the extra money goes to whoever holds the right to import (**quota rent**).

## Worked example

<Worked title="Imports and a tariff" prompt="In Country Z, domestic quantity demanded and supplied of wheat are: at $3, 80 and 40; at $4, 60 and 60. The world price is $3. How much does Z import under free trade? What happens with a $1 tariff?">
  <Step n={1} title="Free trade">
    At the $3 world price, buyers want 80 and domestic farms supply 40. Imports = 80 − 40 = 40.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="With the tariff">
    The domestic price rises to $4. Buyers want 60 and domestic farms supply 60, so imports fall to zero.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Who is affected">
    Farmers sell more at a higher price and gain. Buyers pay more and buy less, and they lose more than farmers gain. There is no tariff revenue, because nothing is imported.
  </Step>
</Worked>

## On the exam

- Find imports or exports at the world price from a graph or table.
- Show the changes in consumer surplus, producer surplus, revenue, and deadweight loss from a tariff or quota.
- Explain who supports a tariff (domestic producers and their workers) and who pays for it (domestic buyers).

<Callout kind="mistake">
Saying free trade makes everyone better off. The country as a whole gains, but some groups lose. With imports, domestic producers lose producer surplus even though total surplus rises.
</Callout>
