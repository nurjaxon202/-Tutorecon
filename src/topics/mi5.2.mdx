Wages rise and fall with labor demand and supply, just as prices do with product demand and supply. The trick is knowing what shifts each curve in a labor market.

## What shifts labor demand

Labor demand is the MRP curve, and MRP = MP × P. So anything that changes the product price or workers’ productivity shifts it:

- **Demand for the product.** Higher demand raises the product price, which raises MRP and shifts labor demand right.
- **Productivity.** Better training or better tools that workers use raise MP, which shifts labor demand right.
- **Prices of other inputs:**
  - If capital is a **complement** to labor (forklifts and warehouse workers), cheaper capital raises labor demand.
  - If capital is a **substitute** for labor (self-checkout machines and cashiers), cheaper capital lowers labor demand.
- **Number of firms** hiring in the market.

A change in the wage itself is a movement along the labor demand curve, not a shift.

## What shifts labor supply

- **Population and immigration.** More working-age people shifts supply right.
- **Wages in other jobs** that use the same skills. Better pay elsewhere pulls workers away and shifts this market’s supply left.
- **Required training or licensing.** Longer or costlier requirements shift supply left.
- **Worker preferences and non-wage conditions:** safety, flexibility, prestige.
- **Government policy**, such as benefits that change how much people want to work.

## Worked example

<Worked title="Two changes at a hospital" prompt="A new drug makes hospital stays shorter, so demand for hospital care falls. At the same time, nursing schools graduate many more nurses. What happens to the wage and number of nurses employed?">
  <Step n={1} title="Demand">
    Less demand for hospital care means lower derived demand for nurses. Labor demand shifts left: wage down, employment down.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Supply">
    More graduates shift labor supply right: wage down, employment up.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Combine">
    Both push the wage down, so it falls. Employment is indeterminate.
  </Step>
</Worked>

## On the exam

- Say whether an event shifts labor demand or labor supply, and which way.
- Decide whether capital is a substitute or a complement for labor in an example.
- Combine shifts and name what is indeterminate, as in any supply and demand problem.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Treating all technology as bad for workers. Technology that is a complement to labor raises workers’ productivity and labor demand. Only technology that replaces workers lowers it.
</Callout>
