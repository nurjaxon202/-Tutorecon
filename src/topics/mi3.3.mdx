In the long run a firm can change everything, including the size of its plant. Long-run costs explain why some industries have a few giant firms and others have thousands of small ones.

## The long-run average total cost curve

The **long-run average total cost (LRATC)** curve shows the lowest average cost of producing each quantity when the firm can pick any plant size. It is traced out by the bottoms of many short-run ATC curves, one for each plant size.

It usually has three sections:

- **Economies of scale:** LRATC falls as the firm grows. Causes include specialization of workers and managers, buying inputs in bulk, and spreading the cost of expensive equipment over more units.
- **Constant returns to scale:** LRATC is flat. Growing does not change average cost.
- **Diseconomies of scale:** LRATC rises. A very large firm gets harder to manage and coordinate, and communication breaks down.

## Returns to scale in numbers

If a firm doubles all of its inputs and output:

- more than doubles, it has **increasing returns to scale** (economies of scale),
- exactly doubles, **constant returns to scale**,
- less than doubles, **decreasing returns to scale** (diseconomies of scale).

## Minimum efficient scale

**Minimum efficient scale** is the smallest output at which LRATC reaches its lowest level. If it is small compared with market demand, many firms can each produce cheaply, and the industry tends to be competitive. If it is large, only a few firms fit, and a natural monopoly or oligopoly is likely.

## Short run versus long run

Diminishing marginal returns (a short-run idea) and diseconomies of scale (a long-run idea) can both push costs up, but for different reasons. Diminishing returns happen because one input is fixed. Diseconomies of scale happen even when every input can change, because the organization itself gets too big.

  <Step n={1} title="Falling LRATC">
    From 100 to 300 units, average cost falls from $10 to $7: economies of scale.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Flat LRATC">
    From 300 to 400 units, average cost stays at $7: constant returns to scale.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Rising LRATC">
    Past 400 units, average cost rises to $8: diseconomies of scale.
  </Step>
  <Step n={4} title="Minimum efficient scale">
    The smallest output with the lowest average cost is 300 units. Firms smaller than this are at a cost disadvantage.
  </Step>

<Callout kind="mistake">
Explaining diseconomies of scale with diminishing marginal returns. Diminishing returns require a fixed input, and in the long run nothing is fixed. Diseconomies come from the difficulty of running a very large organization.
</Callout>
