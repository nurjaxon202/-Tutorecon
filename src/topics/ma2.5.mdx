Inflation is not just a nuisance. It moves wealth between groups when it is unexpected, and it imposes real costs even when everyone sees it coming.

## Unexpected inflation redistributes

When inflation turns out **higher than expected**:

- **Borrowers with fixed-rate loans gain.** They repay with dollars worth less than planned.
- **Lenders lose.** The real value of what they are repaid falls.
- **Savers with fixed returns lose.**
- **People on fixed incomes lose**, such as workers with multiyear contracts or retirees with pensions that are not adjusted for inflation.

When inflation turns out **lower than expected** (or there is unexpected deflation), the effects reverse: lenders gain and borrowers lose.

If inflation is **fully expected**, lenders build it into the nominal interest rate and workers build it into wages, so there is little redistribution. Topic 4.2 shows the math.

## Costs of any inflation

- **Menu costs:** the time and money spent changing prices, reprinting menus, and updating systems.
- **Shoe-leather costs:** the effort of holding less cash and making more trips to the bank to avoid losing value.
- **Unit-of-account costs:** money becomes a less reliable measure, which makes planning and contracts harder and can distort taxes.
- **Uncertainty:** high and variable inflation makes long-term decisions riskier and can discourage investment.

## Hyperinflation

**Hyperinflation** is extremely high inflation, often above 50% a month. It is almost always caused by a government printing money to pay its bills. People stop trusting the currency and switch to barter or foreign money.

## Worked example

  <Step n={1} title="Expected real rate">
    6% − 2% = 4%.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Actual real rate">
    6% − 5% = 1%.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Winner and loser">
    The borrower gains, paying a real rate of 1% instead of 4%. The bank loses.
  </Step>

## On the exam

- Name menu costs and shoe-leather costs from an example.
- Explain why expected inflation causes less redistribution than unexpected inflation.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Saying inflation always hurts borrowers. Unexpected inflation helps borrowers with fixed-rate loans, because they repay in dollars that buy less.
</Callout>
