Most real markets are not perfectly competitive. This topic sorts them into three imperfect structures and explains what they have in common: firms that can set their own price.

## The four market structures

<div class="table-scroll">

| | Perfect competition | Monopolistic competition | Oligopoly | Monopoly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Number of firms | Very many | Many | A few | One |
| Product | Identical | Differentiated | Identical or differentiated | Unique, no close substitutes |
| Entry | Easy | Easy | Hard | Blocked |
| Control over price | None | Some | A lot, but depends on rivals | The most |
| Long-run economic profit | Zero | Zero | Possible | Possible |
| Example | Wheat | Restaurants | Airlines, phone carriers | Local water utility |

</div>

## Market power

A firm has **market power** when it can raise its price without losing all of its customers. That is the same as facing a **downward-sloping demand curve**. Every imperfectly competitive firm has some market power, which means:

- To sell more, it must lower its price, so **marginal revenue is less than price**.
- It still produces where **MR = MC**, but then charges the highest price buyers will pay for that quantity, which is **above MC**.
- Because P > MC, it produces **less than the allocatively efficient quantity**, and there is **deadweight loss**.

## Barriers to entry

Barriers to entry are what let firms keep market power and profit over time:

- **Economies of scale** so large that one or a few firms can supply the market most cheaply
- **Legal barriers:** patents, copyrights, government licenses
- **Control of a key resource**
- **Network effects**, where a product is more useful the more people use it
- **High start-up costs** and strong brand loyalty

## Measuring concentration

A **concentration ratio** is the share of an industry’s output made by its largest firms, often the top four. A high ratio, like 80%, points to an oligopoly.

## On the exam

- Match an example to its market structure.
- Explain why a firm with a downward-sloping demand curve has MR below P.
- Name barriers to entry and say which structure they create.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Assuming only monopolies have market power. Firms in monopolistic competition and oligopoly have downward-sloping demand curves too, so they also set P above MC.
</Callout>
