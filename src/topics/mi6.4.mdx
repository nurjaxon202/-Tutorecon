The same government policy can have different effects depending on the market structure. This topic asks how taxes, subsidies, price controls, and antitrust law change what firms with market power do.

## Lump-sum versus per-unit

The key question for any tax or subsidy on a firm is whether it changes **marginal cost**.

- A **lump-sum** tax or subsidy (a fixed amount, whatever the output) changes **fixed cost** only. ATC shifts, but **MC does not**, so in the short run the profit-maximizing **price and quantity stay the same**. Only profit changes.
- A **per-unit** tax or subsidy changes **marginal cost**. A per-unit tax shifts MC up, so output falls and price rises. A per-unit subsidy shifts MC down, so output rises and price falls.

## In a monopoly

- A **lump-sum tax** lowers the monopolist’s profit but leaves its output and price unchanged.
- A **per-unit tax** raises MC, so the monopolist produces less and charges more.
- A **per-unit subsidy** lowers MC, so the monopolist produces more and charges less, moving toward the efficient quantity.
- A **price ceiling set where MC crosses demand** makes the monopolist act like a competitive firm: it produces the allocatively efficient quantity and deadweight loss disappears. A ceiling set there is the regulator’s “socially optimal price.”

## In perfect competition

In the long run, a **lump-sum tax** on every firm raises ATC, so firms take losses, some exit, and the market price rises until the remaining firms earn zero economic profit again. A per-unit tax shifts each firm’s MC and the market supply curve.

## Antitrust policy

**Antitrust laws** aim to keep markets competitive. Regulators can block mergers that would reduce competition, break up monopolies, and ban collusion and price fixing. The goal is lower prices, more output, and less deadweight loss.

## Worked example

<Worked title="Two taxes on a monopolist" prompt="A profit-maximizing monopolist faces a new $10,000 annual license fee. Later, the government adds a $2 tax on each unit sold instead. How does each change price, output, and profit?">
  <Step n={1} title="License fee">
    A fixed cost. MC is unchanged, so MR = MC at the same quantity. Price and output stay the same. Profit falls by $10,000.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Per-unit tax">
    MC rises by $2. MR = MC now happens at a smaller quantity, so output falls and the price rises. Profit falls.
  </Step>
</Worked>

## On the exam

- Say whether a policy changes fixed or marginal cost, then predict price and quantity.
- Show a price ceiling at MC = D on a monopoly graph and the result.
- Explain what antitrust policy tries to prevent.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Saying a lump-sum tax raises the monopoly price. It does not change marginal cost, so the profit-maximizing quantity and price stay the same. The monopolist just keeps less profit.
</Callout>
