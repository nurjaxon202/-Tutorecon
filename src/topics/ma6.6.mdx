Money moves around the world looking for the best return. This topic connects the loanable funds market to the foreign exchange market through real interest rates and capital flows, a favorite pair of graphs on free-response questions.

## Capital flows follow real interest rates

Other things equal, **financial capital flows toward the country with the higher real interest rate**, because savers there earn more.

If the **US real interest rate rises** relative to other countries:

1. Foreign savers want to buy US assets.
2. In the **foreign exchange market**, demand for dollars rises, so the **dollar appreciates**.
3. In the **US loanable funds market**, the inflow of foreign saving **increases the supply of loanable funds**, which pushes the real interest rate back down somewhat.
4. The capital inflow shows up as a **financial account surplus** in the balance of payments.

## Capital outflows

If a country’s real interest rate falls, or investors lose confidence in it, capital flows **out**. Its currency depreciates, and the supply of loanable funds at home falls, which pushes its real interest rate up. Sudden, large outflows are called **capital flight**.

## Linking the graphs

<div class="table-scroll">

| US real interest rate rises | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Capital flows | Into the US (financial account inflow) |
| Foreign exchange market | Demand for dollars rises, dollar appreciates |
| US loanable funds | Supply rises as foreign saving arrives |
| US net exports | Fall, because the dollar is stronger |

</div>

## Worked example

<Worked title="Rates rise in Mexico" prompt="The real interest rate in Mexico rises relative to the US. What happens to capital flows, the peso, and the dollar?">
  <Step n={1} title="Capital flows">
    Savers move funds toward Mexico to earn the higher return.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Pesos">
    Demand for pesos rises, so the peso appreciates.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Dollars">
    US savers supply dollars to buy pesos, so the dollar depreciates against the peso.
  </Step>
</Worked>

## On the exam

- Predict the direction of capital flows from a change in real interest rates.
- Show the effects on both the foreign exchange graph and the loanable funds graph.
- Record the flow in the financial account.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Using the nominal interest rate. Capital follows the real return, so questions and graphs about capital flows use real interest rates.
</Callout>
