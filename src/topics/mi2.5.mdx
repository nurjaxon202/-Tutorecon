Price is not the only thing that changes what people buy. Two more elasticities measure how demand responds to income and to the price of a different good. The sign of the answer matters as much as the size.

## Income elasticity of demand

**Income elasticity = %ΔQd ÷ %Δincome**

- **Positive: normal good.** People buy more as income rises.
  - Between 0 and 1: a **necessity**. Spending rises, but more slowly than income (groceries, electricity).
  - Above 1: a **luxury**. Spending rises faster than income (travel, jewelry).
- **Negative: inferior good.** People buy less as income rises, usually because they switch to something they like better (store-brand products, used cars for some buyers).

## Cross-price elasticity of demand

**Cross-price elasticity = %ΔQd of good X ÷ %ΔP of good Y**

- **Positive: substitutes.** When Y gets more expensive, people buy more X (Coke and Pepsi).
- **Negative: complements.** When Y gets more expensive, people buy less X (printers and ink).
- **Close to zero: unrelated goods.**

The larger the positive number, the closer the substitutes. Firms use cross-price elasticity to see which rivals they compete with most directly.

## Worked example

<Worked title="Read the signs" prompt="(a) Incomes rise 5% and the quantity of instant noodles demanded falls 3%. (b) The price of printers falls 10% and the quantity of ink demanded rises 15%. Calculate each elasticity and name the relationship.">
  <Step n={1} title="(a) Income elasticity">
    −3% ÷ 5% = −0.6. It is negative, so instant noodles are an inferior good for these buyers.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="(b) Cross-price elasticity">
    Ink quantity changed +15% and printer price changed −10%. 15% ÷ −10% = −1.5. It is negative, so printers and ink are complements.
  </Step>
</Worked>

## On the exam

- Calculate either elasticity and interpret the sign.
- Distinguish necessities (between 0 and 1) from luxuries (above 1).
- Link back to demand shifters: an inferior good’s demand shifts left when income rises; a complement’s higher price shifts demand left.

<Callout kind="mistake">
Dropping the sign. Unlike price elasticity of demand, where AP uses the absolute value, the sign here is the whole point: it tells you normal or inferior, and substitute or complement.
</Callout>
